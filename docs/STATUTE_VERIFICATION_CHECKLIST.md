## Open Decisions for the Team

1. **DMRA Scheduled Conditions vs Unscheduled Cures (VERN-073, 077, 078, 080):**
   - The Drugs and Magic Remedies Act Schedule contains 54 specific diseases/ailments.
   - **Arthritis** (`VERN-073`) is not listed in the Schedule (Row 43 is *Rheumatism*).
   - **Baldness / Hair growth** (`VERN-077`, `VERN-078`) is not on the Schedule at all; Section 4 (false or misleading claims about a drug) must be evaluated as the primary statutory ground.
   - **Obesity bio-resonance therapy** (`VERN-080`): Obesity is Row 38 (spelled *Obsesity* in official text); team must confirm whether Section 3(d) or Section 4 applies to non-drug devices/therapies.

2. **Pan Masala Classification under COTPA (`STAT-002`):**
   - Section 5 of COTPA directly prohibits advertising of tobacco products.
   - Several judicial precedents (including the Delhi High Court in *Vimal Elaichi*) observed that pure pan masala (without tobacco/gutkha) is not technically a tobacco product under COTPA's schedule, and that surrogate restrictions stem from Rule 2(e) or FSSAI Regulations.
   - The team must decide whether pan masala entries (`VERN-015` to `VERN-024`) require an additional or separate FSSAI statutory citation.

3. **CCPA 2022 Clause 6 Proviso (Brand Name Use):**
   - Clause 6 contains an explicit statutory proviso: *mere use of a brand or company name that is also used for a restricted product is not surrogate advertising if the advertisement is not otherwise objectionable.*
   - If an advertiser runs a genuine ad for packaged drinking water with distinct packaging and factual claims, using the brand name alone does not automatically make it illegal.
   - The engineering team must decide how the semantic and regex classifiers account for this proviso rather than flagging every single trademark occurrence.

4. **RBI Digital Lending Directions Applicability:**
   - The RBI Digital Lending Directions (2025) legally bind Regulated Entities (REs) and their Lending Service Providers (LSPs), but do not directly bind third-party publishers or generic advertisers.
   - For generic "No-document loan" social media ads published by unregulated entities, the primary legal hook may be CCPA misleading advertising rather than RBI prudential norms.
   - The team must evaluate whether to research and reference the RBI KYC Master Direction.
